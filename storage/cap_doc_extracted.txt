 Learning Management Systems play an important role in providing students and educators with an organized and accessible platform for managing educational activities. However, traditional methods of managing learning materials, student performance, assignments, and academic progress can be time-consuming, inefficient, and prone to errors. CHAPTER 1
INTRODUCTION
1.1.Project Background and Motivation
Project Background
The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence (AI) and educational technologies as significantly transformed the way educational institutions deliver instruction, manage learning resources, and monitor student performance. Learning Management Systems (LMS) have become essential tools in supporting online and blended learning by providing centralized platforms where students can access course materials, submit academic requirements, participate in assessments, and communicate with instructors. However, conventional LMS platforms primarily focus on content delivery and basic academic record management, with limited capabilities for providing intelligent insights into students’ leaning behaviors, performance, and progress.
At Bestlink College of the Philippines, the increasing use of digital learning environments creates an opportunity to enhance the existing approach to academic monitoring and learning management. As students have different learning capabilities, study habits, levels of engagement, and academic needs, relying solely on conventional methods of monitoring performance may make it difficult for instructors and administrators to identify students who are struggling or falling behind. Students may have limited access to personalized feedback that can help them understand their academic progress and determine which areas require improvement.
The proposed AI-Based Learning Management System for Bestlink College of the Philippines with Integrated Analytics and Progress Training aims to address these challenges by incorporating artificial intelligence, learning analytics, and progress-monitoring features into a centralized learning management platform. The system is designed to support the management and delivery of learning materials while also collecting and analyzing relevant learning data, such as assessment results, activity completion, participation, and other indicators of student engagement. Through these analytics, the system can provide meaningful information regarding individual and overall academic performance.
The integration of AI can further enhance the LMS by enabling intelligent analysis of student performance and learning patterns. Instead of simply recording grades and completed activities, the proposed system can use available academic and behavioral data to generate insights that may help identify areas of difficulty, monitor learning progress, and provide appropriate recommendations. These capabilities can assist instructors in making more informed decisions and enable students to become more aware of their strengths, weaknesses, and learning progress.
The system will also incorporate progress training, which focuses on helping students improve continuously through structured learning activities, assessments, feedback, and progress monitoring. Rather than treating learning as a process that ends with the submission of requirements or completion of examinations, the proposed system promotes continuous development by allowing students to track their performance and work toward identified learning objectives. This approach can encourage students to take a more active role in managing their own learning.
Integrated analytics can provide instructors and academic administrators with a broader view of students performance. Data presented through dashboards and reports can help stakeholders monitor trends, identify potential academic concerns, evaluate student engagement, and support evidence-based academic interventions. By consolidating learning management and analytics into one system, the proposed platform can reduce dependence on fragmented tools and manual monitoring processes.
Project Motivation
The motivation for developing the proposed system stems from the need to make learning management more intelligent, personalized, data-driven, and responsive to students needs. Traditional LMS platforms are effective in organizing educational resources and activities, but they may not sufficiently address the growing demand for personalized learning support and timely academic intervention. The increasing availability of AI technologies provides an opportunity to extend the functionality of an LMS beyond basic course management toward a system capable of generating actionable learning insights.
For students, the system is motivated by the need for greater visibility into their academic progress. Students may find it difficult to determine whether they are meeting learning expectations when information is limited to grades, scores, and completed requirements. Through progress tracking and AI-supported insights, the proposed system can present students with clearer information about their performance and areas that may require additional attention. This can encourage self-directed learning, continuous improvement, and greater engagement with academic activities.
For instructors, the motivation is to provide tools that can simplify and improve the process of monitoring students. Managing large numbers of students can make it challenging to consistently identify performance patterns and students who may need additional assistance. Integrated analytics can help instructors interpret relevant learning data and identify students or learning areas that warrant attention. This can support more timely and targeted academic interventions while reducing the burden associated with manual monitoring and reporting.
For academic administrators, the system can provide centralized and organized information that may support academic planning and decision-making. Aggregated analytics can offer insights into student performance and engagement across courses or learning activities, allowing administrators to better understand areas of concern and potential opportunities for improvement.
The project is motivated by the goal of developing a learning management environment that does not merely store academic information but transforms learning data into useful insights and actionable support. By combining AI, learning management, analytics, and progress training, the proposed system seeks to create a more adaptive and student-centered educational platform for Bestlink College of the Philippines.

RELATED LITERATURE, STUDIES, AND TECHNICAL BACKGROUND
2.1 Review of Related Literature
Learning Management Systems (LMS) have become an important part of modern higher education because they provide a centralized platform where learning materials, assessments, student activities, grades, and communication can be managed. As schools increasingly use digital platforms for teaching and learning, LMS platforms have also started generating large amounts of information about how students interact with their courses. This information can be used to better understand student participation, performance, and academic progress. Recent studies show that LMS platforms are gradually developing beyond simple repositories for learning materials and are becoming more integrated environments that include learning analytics, personalization, assessment, and artificial intelligence (AI). A 2026 systematic review of LMS and learning analytics in higher education found that LMS implementation is increasingly associated with student engagement and academic outcomes. Learning Management Systems in Higher Education
The use of Learning Management Systems in higher education is particularly important for institutions that need an organized and accessible way of managing academic activities. An LMS can provide students and instructors with a common platform for accessing learning materials, lessons, assignments, quizzes, grades, announcements, and other course-related activities. By bringing these functions together, an LMS can reduce the need to use several separate platforms and can create a more consistent learning experience.
The usefulness of an LMS, however, goes beyond simply providing access to course materials. The system can also record information about student activities, such as participation, assessment performance, and interaction with learning resources. These records can help instructors understand how students are progressing and where additional support may be needed. The 2026 systematic review published by Social Sciences & Humanities Open emphasizes that LMS technology should be viewed as part of a wider socio-technical learning environment rather than as an independent technological tool. This suggests that an effective LMS should be designed around the actual needs of students and instructors and should support the educational processes of an institution.
Online Learning and Student Engagement
Student engagement is another important consideration in online learning. Engagement involves several dimensions, including how actively students participate in activities, how they think about and process learning materials, how they feel about the learning experience, and how they interact with others. Bergdahl et al. (2024), in their systematic review of 159 learning analytics studies in higher education, found that many studies measure student engagement using observable activities such as clicks, system interactions, and the amount of time spent on tasks. However, the researchers pointed out that these measures alone may not fully represent the different dimensions of student engagement.
This finding is relevant to LMS development because student participation should not be judged only by the number of times a student logs in or clicks on a resource. Other indicators can provide a clearer picture of learning behavior, including assignment completion, quiz performance, access to lessons and learning materials, course progression, and interaction with course activities. Combining these indicators can give students and instructors a more meaningful understanding of engagement and academic progress.
Learning Analytics in LMS
The integration of Learning Analytics (LA) into LMS platforms provides a way to transform student activity data into useful information. Learning analytics generally involves collecting, measuring, analyzing, and interpreting data related to learners and their learning environment. Ifenthaler and Yau (2020) explained that learning analytics can support study success in higher education by helping identify patterns related to student progress and possible learning difficulties. Bergdahl et al. (2024) likewise noted that learning analytics can be used to monitor progression, examine learner behavior, predict performance, provide feedback, and support appropriate interventions.
These capabilities are closely related to the proposed system because the project aims to integrate analytics into an LMS to help students and instructors understand academic performance and progress. Instead of simply storing activity records, the system can process relevant information and present it in a form that is easier to understand and use for decision-making.
Student Progress Monitoring and Early Identification
Learning analytics can also help instructors monitor student progress and identify possible learning difficulties at an earlier stage. An LMS can collect information such as completed lessons, submitted assignments, quiz scores, grades, login activity, and course completion. When these data are analyzed together, they can provide an overview of whether students are meeting expected learning requirements.
According to Ifenthaler and Yau (2020), learning analytics can support study success by providing information that helps institutions understand learner progress and identify students who may require additional assistance. This demonstrates how an LMS can move beyond simply recording academic activities and become a tool for providing meaningful support. In relation to the proposed system, the progress-tracking component can use academic activity data to show students their current learning status while also giving instructors information that can help them determine when intervention or additional guidance may be appropriate.
Learning Analytics Dashboards
Learning analytics dashboards are another important feature of data-driven LMS platforms. These dashboards present learning information visually, allowing students and instructors to understand performance and progress more quickly. Paulsen and Lindsay (2024), in their review of student-facing learning analytics dashboards in higher education, identified a growing focus on dashboards that do more than display information. Modern dashboards are increasingly designed to support reflection, feedback, and appropriate learning actions.
This supports the inclusion of analytics dashboards in the proposed system. A well-designed dashboard can present information such as course progress, assessment results, assignment completion, grades, and other learning indicators in a clear and understandable manner. For students, this can make it easier to recognize areas where they are performing well and areas that may require more attention. For instructors, the dashboard can provide a broader view of student performance and progress.
Learning Analytics and User Experience
Integrating analytics into an LMS can also contribute to the overall user experience of online learning. This iterative approach helped ensure that the Online Learning and LMS subsystem remained aligned with the requirements of the proposed AI-Based Learning Management System for Bestlink College of the Philippines with Integrated Analytics and Progress Training, while also supporting continuous improvement throughout the development process (Pressman & Maxim, 2020).
3.1.3 Scrum Artifacts 
The Scrum Artifacts applied in the development of the Design and Development of an AI-Based Learning Management System for Bestlink College of the Philippines with Integrated Analytics and Progress Training served as essential tools for organizing system requirements, planning development activities, tracking progress, and ensuring that each sprint produced a functional improvement to the Learning Management System (LMS). According to Schwaber and Sutherland (2020), Scrum artifacts represent work or value and are designed to maximize transparency, allowing the Scrum Team and stakeholders to have a shared understanding of the product's current status and progress. The three primary Scrum artifacts—the Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, and Increment—were applied to guide the development of LMS features such as course management, learning materials, assignments, quizzes, grading, progress tracking, notifications, and analytics. The Product Backlog contained the prioritized requirements and desired features of the LMS, while the Sprint Backlog identified the specific LMS functionalities selected for development during each sprint. The Increment represented the completed and tested features delivered at the end of each sprint, ensuring that the system continuously progressed toward the overall goal of providing an effective online learning platform (Schwaber & Sutherland, 2020).

3.2 Architectural Design Decisions
The Design and Development of an AI-Based Learning Management System for Bestlink College of the Philippines with Integrated Analytics and Progress Tracking was carefully planned to provide an organized, scalable, and maintainable platform for online learning. The architectural design was based on the needs of the system's primary users, including students, teachers, and administrators, as well as the functional requirements of the Online Learning and Learning Management System. Each module is designed to perform a specific function while remaining connected to other modules to support the overall learning process. This approach makes the system easier to develop, maintain, update, and expand as the educational needs of the institution and its users change.
The system architecture considers the proper flow of information between the major modules, including User and Role Management, Learning Materials, Lessons and Modules, Assignments, Online Quizzes, Virtual Class Integration, Grade Management, Feedback and Comments, Progress and Module Completion Tracking, Notifications, and Learning Analytics. These modules work together to allow teachers to manage courses and learning materials, students to access lessons and complete learning activities, and administrators to monitor system and academic information. The architecture ensures that data such as course activities, assessment results, grades, and student progress can be properly recorded, updated, and accessed by authorized users.
3.2.1 Why Microservices? Justify the choice over Monolithic architecture.
We chose Microservices Architecture because our proposed AI-Based Learning Management System (LMS) consists of several functional modules that handle different learning and academic processes. Each module has a specific purpose and can be developed as an independent service. Using microservices allows each service to be developed, tested, updated, and maintained separately without significantly affecting other parts of the system. This approach helps make the development process more organized and reduces the risk of system-wide issues when changes or updates are introduced.
Compared to Monolithic Architecture, where all LMS functions are combined into a single application, Microservices Architecture provides greater flexibility, scalability, and maintainability. For the proposed system, the Integrated Analytics and Progress Tracking features can process learning and performance data separately, while the AI-based components can provide personalized learning recommendations and support student progress monitoring. If Bestlink College of the Philippines decides to add new learning features or improve existing modules in the future, developers can modify or expand the specific service without rebuilding the entire system. This architecture also allows different parts of the LMS to be developed and improved independently, making it suitable for a system that is expected to continuously grow and adapt to the changing needs of students, instructors, and administrators.
3.2.2 Core Architectural Patterns Used 
The AI-Based Learning Management System for Bestlink College of the Philippines with Integrated Analytics and Progress Training uses architectural patterns to promote modularity, maintainability, scalability, and efficient management of learning data. These patterns help organize the different components of the system and ensure that students, instructors, and administrators can access and manage learning-related functions effectively.
The system follows a client-server architecture, where users access the Learning Management System through a web browser, while the server manages business logic, data processing, authentication, and communication with the database. This architecture allows learning data such as courses, lessons, assignments, quizzes, grades, attendance, and student progress to be processed and managed centrally. It also provides better control over user access and helps ensure that learning resources and records are handled securely.
The system also applies the Model-View-Controller (MVC) pattern, which is supported by the Laravel Framework. The Model manages learning-related data and database operations, the View presents information and interfaces to students, instructors, and administrators, while the Controller processes user requests and coordinates system operations. Separating these responsibilities keeps the application organized and makes it easier to develop, test, update, and maintain different LMS functions.
In addition, the system uses API Architecture to facilitate communication between system components and external services. APIs allow standardized data exchange and support the integration of AI-based features, such as student progress analysis, learning recommendations, and intelligent activity suggestions. Through APIs, relevant learning data can be processed by AI services and the resulting insights or recommendations can be returned to the LMS for presentation to users.
By combining these architectural patterns, the proposed AI-Based Learning Management System achieves a structured and maintainable architecture that supports online learning, academic management, integrated analytics, and progress tracking. This approach improves code organization and system performance while providing a flexible foundation for future enhancements and additional AI-driven learning features at Bestlink College of the Philippines.
3.3 Development, Operations, and Quality Assurance (QA) Methodology
The development, operations, and quality assurance (QA) methodology for the Online Learning and Learning Management System (LMS) combines DevOps practices and software testing to ensure that the system is reliable, secure, and functions as intended. By integrating development, testing, and operations processes, the development team can efficiently implement, test, and deploy LMS features such as course management, learning materials, assignments, online quizzes, grading, progress tracking, and integrated analytics, while maintaining the overall stability and performance of the system.
This phase of the project focuses on organizing the development and deployment processes while minimizing repetitive tasks through automation and structured workflows. It also includes securing the database configuration during local development and testing the system's web pages and API endpoints to verify proper communication between the frontend and backend components. The QA process uses appropriate testing tools to evaluate backend functions, API responses, database operations, and the system's user interface. These tests help ensure that the student, teacher, and administrator features of the LMS operate correctly and consistently when handling different amounts of learning materials, assessments, student records, user activities, and progress data.
3.3.1 DevOps Toolchain 
The Online Learning and Learning Management System (LMS) applies DevOps practices to manage software development and version control. As the sole developer, the developer uses Git to track changes made to the application's source code and manage different versions of the LMS. Git maintains a history of code modifications, allowing the developer to review changes, prevent code loss, and restore previous versions when necessary. This supports the development and maintenance of important LMS features such as course management, learning materials, assignments, online quizzes, grading, progress tracking, and analytics.
The developer also uses GitHub to store and manage the source code of the Online Learning and LMS. GitHub provides a centralized repository where the developer can organize the project's code, monitor updates, and maintain different versions of the application. The combined use of Git and GitHub provides an organized DevOps workflow that supports continuous development, version control, testing, and system maintenance. Through these tools, the developer can efficiently manage the LMS and maintain its reliability while continuously improving its learning features and functionality.
3.3.2 CI/CD Pipeline Design
The Online Learning and Learning Management System (LMS) follows a Continuous Integration and Continuous Deployment (CI/CD) process to manage code changes and deploy updated versions of the system in an organized and efficient manner. This process supports the continuous development, testing, and improvement of the LMS features and functionalities.
During the development stage, new LMS features, enhancements, and bug fixes are developed and tested in the development environment. Once the changes have been reviewed and confirmed to be functioning properly, the updated source code is committed to Git and pushed to the project's GitHub repository. Git is used to track changes and maintain different versions of the source code, while GitHub provides a centralized repository that supports organized code management and collaboration.
After the updated code is committed to the repository, the latest version of the Online Learning and LMS application is deployed to the designated hosting environment for further testing and evaluation. This allows the developers to verify that newly implemented features, such as learning materials, assignments, quizzes, grading, progress tracking, and analytics, function properly within the system.
Overall, the CI/CD process used in the Online Learning and LMS is simple but effective in supporting continuous development. By integrating Git and GitHub with the deployment process, the development workflow becomes more organized, allowing the developer to track changes, manage source code versions, test updates, and continuously improve the system throughout its development.
3.3.3 Testing Strategy 
Testing will be conducted on the proposed Online Learning and Learning Management System (LMS) to ensure that its functions work properly and meet the functional and technical requirements of the intended users. For the mockup testing, the proposed system interfaces, workflows, and features will be reviewed and evaluated during the development process. Any errors, inconsistencies, or usability issues identified during testing will be documented and addressed before the final implementation of the system.
Unit Testing: To verify whether the features and modules, such as Learning Materials, Assignments, Online Quizzes, Grade Management, Progress Tracking and Notifications, function according to their intended requirements.
System Testing: To evaluate the overall functionality, performance, reliability, usability, and security of the proposed Online Learning and LMS as a complete system.
Integration Testing: To determine whether the different LMS modules work and communicate properly when integrated, particularly the connection between courses, learning materials, assignments, quizzes, grades, and student progress.
API Testing: To verify the communication between the LMS and its connected services or APIs, ensuring that data can be properly requested, transmitted, and received.
Security Testing: To ensure that the proposed LMS can protect student and teacher accounts, academic records, learning materials, grades, and other important information from unauthorized access and common security threats.
3.4 Innovation Framework
The development and implementation of the AI-Based Learning Management System with Integrated Analytics and Progress Tracking is guided by an innovation framework that focuses on using digital technologies to improve the delivery and management of online learning. The framework aims to provide a more organized, accessible, and efficient learning environment that will help students, teachers, and administrators manage courses, learning materials, academic activities, and student progress through a centralized platform.
While existing learning processes may rely on face-to-face instruction, printed materials, separate files, and traditional methods of monitoring student performance, the proposed innovation introduces a centralized web-based Learning Management System (LMS) that integrates essential online learning processes. These include Learning Material Management, Assignment Submission, Online Quizzes, Virtual Class Integration, Grading and Feedback, Student Progress Tracking, Notifications, and Learning Analytics. Through these integrated modules, learning resources and academic records can be stored and accessed more efficiently by authorized users, reducing the use of scattered records and improving the overall management of online learning activities.
The innovation framework also integrates AI-Based Learning Support, Integrated Analytics, and Progress Tracking into the Learning Management System. Integrated Analytics allows the system to generate organized information and reports based on students' grades, quiz results, assignment performance, course completion, and learning activities. Progress Tracking enables students and teachers to monitor learning progress and identify areas that may require improvement. On the other hand, AI-Based Learning Support can provide personalized learning recommendations based on students' performance, learning activities, and progress. These features can help teachers better understand student learning patterns while allowing students to receive more relevant learning resources and recommendations based on their individual needs.
